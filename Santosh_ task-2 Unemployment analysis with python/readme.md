# 📊 Unemployment Rate Analysis – Exploratory Data Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project focuses on performing **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)** on unemployment data to understand unemployment patterns and trends across different regions and time periods.

The analysis examines regional differences, monthly trends, changes over time, the impact of the COVID-19 period, and relationships between unemployment, employment, and labour participation rates.

The project uses data visualization and statistical analysis to identify meaningful patterns and insights from the dataset.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

The main objectives of this project are:

* Analyze and understand the unemployment dataset.
* Inspect the dataset structure, shape, missing values, and data types.
* Study region-wise average unemployment rates.
* Analyze month-wise unemployment trends.
* Compare unemployment rates over time for at least three major states or regions.
* Identify the top 10 states with the highest average unemployment rates.
* Analyze the correlation between unemployment rate, employment, and labour participation rate.
* Compare unemployment rates between pre-COVID and post-COVID periods.
* Present clear written observations for each visualization.

## 📂 Dataset

The project uses an unemployment dataset containing information about employment and unemployment conditions across different regions and dates.

### Important Variables

* **Region** – Name of the state or region.
* **Date** – Date of the recorded observation.
* **Estimated Unemployment Rate (%)** – Estimated percentage of unemployed people.
* **Estimated Employed** – Estimated number of employed people.
* **Estimated Labour Participation Rate (%)** – Percentage of the population participating in the labour force.

---

# 🔍 Exploratory Data Analysis

## 1. Data Loading and Inspection

The dataset is loaded and inspected to understand its structure and contents.

The analysis includes:

* Number of rows and columns.
* Column names.
* Data types.
* Sample records.
* Missing or null values.

The date column is converted into an appropriate date format to support time-series analysis.

### Observation

The initial inspection provides an understanding of the dataset structure and helps identify any missing values or incorrect data types before performing further analysis.

---

## 2. Region-wise Average Unemployment Rate

The average unemployment rate is calculated for each region to compare unemployment levels across different states.

### Observation

The analysis shows that unemployment rates vary across regions. Some states have considerably higher average unemployment rates than others, indicating differences in employment conditions across regions.

---

## 3. Month-wise Unemployment Trends

The unemployment data is analyzed month by month to identify changes and possible patterns throughout the year.

### Observation

The month-wise analysis shows fluctuations in unemployment rates across different months. Some months experience relatively higher unemployment rates, while others show lower rates, suggesting possible seasonal or time-related variations.

---

## 4. Time-Series Analysis

A time-series line chart is used to compare unemployment rates over time for at least three major states or regions.

### Observation

The time-series analysis shows that unemployment rates fluctuate over time and that different regions follow different patterns. Changes during the COVID-19 period are particularly noticeable, reflecting the effect of the pandemic on employment conditions.

---

## 5. Top 10 States with Highest Average Unemployment Rate

The states are ranked according to their average unemployment rates, and the top 10 states with the highest rates are visualized.

### Observation

The analysis identifies the states with the highest average unemployment rates during the study period. The differences between these states demonstrate that unemployment levels were not uniform across regions.

---

## 6. Correlation Analysis

A correlation heatmap is used to study the relationship between:

* Unemployment Rate
* Employment
* Labour Participation Rate

### Observation

The correlation analysis helps identify positive and negative relationships between employment-related variables. A positive correlation indicates that two variables tend to increase together, while a negative correlation indicates that one variable tends to decrease as the other increases.

Correlation shows association between variables and does not necessarily indicate a causal relationship.

---

## 7. Pre-COVID vs Post-COVID Comparison

The dataset is divided into pre-COVID and post-COVID periods based on the date. The average unemployment rates for both periods are compared.

### Observation

The comparison helps identify how unemployment conditions changed between the pre-COVID and post-COVID periods. It provides an overall view of the changes in unemployment associated with the COVID-19 period.
